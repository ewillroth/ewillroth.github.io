Guide & FAQ
How fasting works — metabolic states ▼

When you fast, your body progresses through distinct metabolic phases. The longer you go, the deeper the benefit.

0 – 4 hrs
Fed StateDigesting your last meal. Insulin is elevated, fat burning is paused.
4 – 12 hrs
Early FastBlood glucose normalizes. Body starts tapping glycogen stores for energy.
12 – 18 hrs
Fat BurningGlycogen depletes. Fat burning (lipolysis) ramps up significantly.
18 – 24 hrs
Ketosis + AutophagyDeep ketosis begins. Autophagy peaks — your cells start breaking down and recycling damaged proteins and dead cells.
24 – 72 hrs
Deep RepairGrowth hormone spikes significantly, preserving muscle. Deep cellular repair continues throughout.
Tips for getting through a fast ▼
→
Black coffee is your best friend. It suppresses appetite, boosts fat oxidation, and gives you a mental lift without breaking the fast.
→
Sparkling water during hunger waves. Topo Chico, Pellegrino — the carbonation creates a feeling of fullness. Keep it cold.
→
Electrolytes kill hunger. Sodium especially — a pinch of sea salt in water or a zero-calorie electrolyte packet works surprisingly well.
→
Hunger is wave-based. If you feel it, wait 20 minutes. It almost always passes on its own.
→
Reframe hunger. It's a signal that fat burning is happening — not a problem to solve. Let it pass.
→
Stay busy. Hunger is loudest when you're idle. Schedule demanding work or exercise during your fasted hours.
→
Zero Monster / zero-cal drinks. No calories, no meaningful insulin response. Fine to use during a fast, though black coffee is cleaner long-term.
What breaks a fast? ▼
✓
These do NOT break your fast: Water, black coffee, plain tea, sparkling water, electrolytes (no sugar), creatine, most zero-calorie drinks.
✗
These WILL break your fast: Any calories, milk or cream in coffee, sweetened drinks, protein shakes, BCAAs, gum with sugar, most supplements with fillers.
~
Gray area: Artificial sweeteners (sucralose etc.) don't have calories but may trigger a minor insulin response in some people. Functionally fine for most.
Creatine while fasting ▼

Creatine has no calories and doesn't trigger an insulin response — it won't break your fast.

Absorption is slightly more efficient when insulin is elevated (i.e. after eating), so the optimal time is right when you break your fast, with your meal. For standard daily dosing the difference is negligible — consistency matters more than timing.

Keep your dose the same (3–5g/day). No adjustment needed for fasting. Just stay well hydrated — creatine and fasting both put mild demand on hydration.

How to save this app to your desktop ▼

This is a self-contained HTML file — no install needed. Save it once and it works forever offline.

1
Download the fileRight-click the download link from Claude and save as fasting-timer.html somewhere permanent (e.g. your Desktop or Documents folder).
2
Open in Chrome or BraveDouble-click the file. It opens in your default browser. If it doesn't open in Chrome, right-click → Open with → Chrome.
3
Create a desktop shortcut (opens as standalone app)Right-click your desktop → New → Shortcut. In the location field paste:

"C:\Program Files\Google\Chrome\Application\chrome.exe" --app=file:///C:/Users/YOUR_NAME/Desktop/fasting-timer.html

Replace YOUR_NAME with your Windows username. Name it Fasting Timer and click Finish.
4
Using Brave instead?Same steps, just change the path to:

"C:\Program Files\Brave Software\Brave-Browser\Application\brave.exe" --app=file:///C:/Users/YOUR_NAME/Desktop/fasting-timer.html
5
Pin to taskbarAfter opening via the shortcut, right-click the taskbar icon → Pin to taskbar. One click to open from now on.

Your schedule settings are saved in the browser's local storage — they persist between sessions as long as you open the file in the same browser.
